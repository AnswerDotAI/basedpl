# The cases that `tests/speed.rs` times in the native build and `tests/wasm-speed.mjs` times in the browser build. Each case line holds the code, two native budgets and a wasm budget, separated by tabs. The first native budget is for macOS on Apple silicon, where matrix products and decompositions run on Accelerate. The second is for Linux on CI's x86 runner, where they run on rustymath's own kernels. `rustymath::timing::NATIVE_COLUMN` picks the native budget a machine checks. Each section starts with its name in brackets and the prefs its session starts with. Every session then runs the lines under the `[setup]` before its section.
# Budgets in `[copies]`, `[exact]`, `[images]`, `[objectives]`, `[calls]`, `[derivatives]` and `[structure]` count copies of the `n` items that the section's setup makes. A budget in `[widths]` is the most that the time in that section's 32-bit session may be, as a fraction of the time in the `[exact]` session. It is 0.25 more than the fraction measured where the budget applies: on a Mac for the macOS budget, on CI's runner for the Linux budget, and the worse of the two in the browser module for the wasm budget.
# A native budget in copies is twice the most the case has cost on its own platform: on a Mac for the macOS budget, on CI's runner for the Linux budget. A wasm budget in copies is twice the most the case has cost on a Mac and on CI's runner, in the module that `cargo wasm` builds. Each budget in copies is at least 0.5.
# A case in copies that CI hasn't timed yet has a Linux budget of four times its Mac cost, timed with `native=True` where it runs matrix products or decompositions, and a wasm budget of four times its Mac cost in the browser module. A `[widths]` case that CI hasn't timed yet has its Mac fraction plus 0.25. The next CI run that logs every cost, a push's `Test` run or a `Speed` run, sets their budgets under the rules above.
